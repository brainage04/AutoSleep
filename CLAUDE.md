# AutoSleep: notes for agents

- Target: Minecraft 1.21.11, Fabric, client-only (servers need nothing). Decompiled source: `~/agent-work/mc-src/1.21.11/merged/` (see its README).
- Vanilla decides beds by the `BED_RULE` environment attribute (`BedBlock#useWithoutItem`, `ServerPlayer#startSleepInBed`). It is not synced to clients (`EnvironmentAttributes.java:105`), so the client level always reads the default rule; the `AutoSleeper` class comment explains the singleplayer and remote routes.
- Behaviours that must survive, with the GameTest step that covers each:
  - uses a bed only when the player's crosshair is a block hit on it and from vanilla reach: the mod puts the view on the bed, then on the next tick uses the game's own crosshair pick, never a synthesised hit result, so the server has the new view before the use: `out-of-reach`, `in-reach`, `nearest-bed-blue`, `nearest-bed-red` (their `UseBlockCallback` handler checks the crosshair, the reach and both interaction ranges on the client, and the player's view as the server has it), `crosshair-short-of-the-bed`
  - starts walking or turns to the bed on the first tick the game lets the player sleep, not at the next retry, and does nothing at all by day: every step of `AutoSleepClientGameTest` (by day it asserts no move, no turn and no use for 30 ticks plus 10 of dusk; at nightfall it asserts the walk or the use within 2 ticks)
  - never walks to or uses a Nether or End bed: `*-the_nether-bed`, `*-the_end-bed`
  - never uses a bed whose rule explodes, even when it allows sleep (the `explodes()` check on the integrated server's answer): `singleplayer-exploding_beds-bed`
  - follows a datapack dimension's own rule through the integrated server, not the Overworld's key: `singleplayer-allowed_beds-bed`
  - uses the integrated server's answer only at the next attempt, for the same bed (`singleplayer-exploding_biome-bed`) in the same level (`singleplayer-other_dimension-bed`); never after an attempt that asked nothing (`singleplayer-stale_answer-bed`), after sleeping (`singleplayer-overworld-bed`) or in the next night (`singleplayer-next_night-bed`)
  - on a server, never uses a bed in a non-`minecraft` dimension type: `dedicated-exploding_beds-bed`, `dedicated-allowed_beds-bed`
  - sleeps at night in the Overworld, in the nearest bed, and stays up after the night is skipped: `out-of-reach`, `in-reach`, `nearest-bed-blue`, `nearest-bed-red`, `*-overworld-bed`
  - a player who leaves the bed or is woken at night stays up in that dimension of that world until the game refuses sleep there or a later day starts anywhere in that world, also across a trip to the Nether and a reconnect (`stays-up-after-leaving-the-bed`, which also holds the Overworld's time back 5 ticks on the way back so the new level ticks at day time 0, as `TIME_SYNC_TICKS` allows for, and `stay-up-paused`, where a pause before that time arrives does not count towards `TIME_SYNC_TICKS`); other dimensions and worlds are not held (`stays-up-in-its-own-dimension`, `stays-up-in-its-own-world`), and a new day seen in the Nether ends it (`stay-up-ends-the-next-day`); every bed-rule step therefore starts its night from a day
  - every tick counter (`cooldownTicks`, `levelTicks`, `notSafeTicks`, `walkTicks`) counts through `AutoSleeper#counts` on a named `Clock`, stated where the counter is declared; no clock counts a tick while a singleplayer game is paused (`walk-paused`, `walk-paused-overlay`, `turn-paused-overlay`, `monster-wait-paused`, `stay-up-paused`); `stayUp` and `notSafe` end by day number, not by a tick count
  - tries a bed `TRIES_PER_BED` times a night, then tells the player once and tries the next nearest bed: a missed look (`bed-under-the-floor`, `crosshair-short-of-the-bed`) or a use the server refused (`server-refuses-the-bed`); a bed Baritone finds no path to (`PathEvent.CALC_FAILED`, `bed-in-a-pit`) or does not reach within `WALK_DEADLINE_TICKS` of ticks without a screen open and not paused (`walk-never-arrives`, `walk-paused`, `walk-paused-overlay`) is given up at once
  - a refusal for monsters nearby is not a failed try: the mod keeps to that bed, which has the respawn point, and uses no other bed in that world that night, also across a reconnect and a dimension trip (`monster-reconnect`, which also holds the first bed's chunk back after the reconnect so the client reads air there until it arrives, and `monster-other-dimension`); it tries that bed every `NOT_SAFE_RETRY_TICKS` (`monsters-nearby`), gives it up with one message after `NOT_SAFE_GIVE_UP_TICKS`, counted with a screen open but not while paused (`monster-stays`, `monster-wait-paused`), never walks the player back to it (`monster-walk-away`), and forgets it once it is broken (`monster-bed-broken`)
  - Baritone: `chatControl` off (`chat-reaches-the-server`); `allowBreak`, `allowPlace` and `allowWaterBucketFall` off only during the mod's own walk and given back afterwards (`own-baritone-goal`, `bed-in-a-pit`), also when the walk switches beds (`walk-switches-beds`); no walk while a Baritone task of the player's is active (`own-baritone-goal`); a walk whose goal another mod replaced is never cancelled (`goal-from-another-mod`)
  - every Baritone command is wrapped (`PlayerCommand`), re-entrantly, because an alias runs its target inside it: a command while the mod walks or waits out monsters makes the player stay up for the night, with one message, also when more commands follow (`stop-ends-the-walk`, `monster-stop`, `mine-during-the-walk`), and the command sees the player's own `chatControl`, so `baritone/settings.txt` never holds the mod's values (`settings-file`, also through `#modified` and `#reset all`) and keeps the player's own `chatControl` change (`own-chat-control`)
  - never uses the bed while sneaking with an item in either hand, checked at the turn (`sneaking-with-blocks`) and again at the click (`sneak-before-the-click`), with a screen open (`screen-open`) or in spectator mode (`spectator`)
  - a standalone Baritone jar (no `baritone.api`) turns the mod off with a chat message instead of crashing: `BaritoneMissingTest` (unit test)
- The showcase scene is a saved structure, `src/gametest/resources/data/autosleep_test/structure/bedroom.nbt` (an oak plank floor, the red bed's head at (0, surface, 8) and the blue bed's at (10, surface, 8), facing south); edit it with a structure block, and keep `SCENE_CORNER_FROM_RED_BED` in step. Each scenario is placed and set to day before its step marker, so nothing is set up on camera.
- Film-only code lives in `src/recording` (`Showcase`, the two HUD panels and the `AutoSleepRecordingClient` entrypoint): the renderer check of a take, the bed framing check, the chat clearing and the closing hold. The GameTests call it, and `build.gradle` loads it as part of the GameTest mod. Anything a test asserts about the mod stays in `src/gametest`.
- Tests and recording, from the repository root: `nomad-heavy --host server2 --mem 10G --lock "$PWD/.gametest.lock" --lock /tmp/autosleep-dedicated-server.lock --lock /tmp/gpu-recording-queue.lock --checkout "$PWD" --fetch build/recording/ --fetch build/run/clientGameTest/logs/ --fetch build/libs/ -- env AUTOSLEEP_RECORDING_DISPLAY=:1 scripts/record-client-gametest.sh` (measured on thomas-server, 11 Nov 2026: 3.9-5G peak, about 4-5 minutes; `--host server2` because the GPU display for recordings is Xorg `:1` there, and `AUTOSLEEP_RECORDING_DISPLAY=:1` makes the take draw on it under the display lock and fail if the client's OpenGL renderer is not NVIDIA). Without a recording: `nomad-heavy --mem 8G --lock "$PWD/.gametest.lock" --checkout "$PWD" -- xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 1920x1080x24" ./gradlew --no-daemon runClientGameTest` (no `--quick`: client runs are not quick jobs).
- Cut and measure the showcase after fetching `build/recording/`: `scripts/finalize-recording.sh <tag> <peak memory>` writes the video and its sidecar into `~/agent-work/recordings-<date>/` and prints the audio levels and the new-images-per-second measurement. `scripts/trim-scenarios.py` is the cutter: it needs the `[CLIENT_GAMETEST_RECORDER]` lines, fades every cut 30 ms, and raises the sound by one linear gain (the smaller of -20 LUFS and a -2 dBFS true peak), never a compressor or limiter.
- The capture route is at unity: the game's stream sits in the null sink at 0.00 dB (see `audio-sink-inputs.txt`), and ffmpeg records the monitor as 32-bit float (`-c:a pcm_f32le` before `-i`; the pulse input's default is 16-bit, which leaves footsteps at -42 dBFS a few bits of detail). The mix is quiet because these scenes only play footsteps, at 0.15 volume (`Entity.java:1328`), so never "fix" the level with a fixed gain in the capture. Grass steps are mostly hiss (92 % of their energy above 4 kHz, against 0.2 % for wood steps), so a walk on grass raised to programme level is bursts of hiss; the scene's plank floor gives wood steps.
